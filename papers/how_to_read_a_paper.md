# How To Read a Paper 

## Summary

Outline a three pass method for reading research papers.

## Introduction
Instead of reading a research paper in one go, read it in three passes,
understanding the paper at different levels each pass and determining whether
the next pass is required. The first pass should give you a general idea of the
paper. The second pass should give you a grasp of the paper's contents but not
the details. The third pass helps you understand the paper in depth.

## First pass
This should give you a bird's eye view of the paper. It should take five to ten
minutes and you should
- Read the title, abstract, and introduction
- Read the section and subsection headings
- Read the conclusion
- Read references, noting things you've read

At the end of the first pass, you should be able to answer
- Category: What type of paper is this? A measurement, analysis, description?
- Context: Which other papers is it related to
- Correctness: Do assumptions appear to be valid?
- Contributions: What are the paper's main contributions?
- Clariy: Is the paper well written

## Second pass
Read the paper more carefully but ignoring specific details like proofs. This
pass should take from an hour to two hours. You should be able to grasp the
content of the paper and summarize the main ideas. You should
- Understand the main point(s) of each paragraph
- Look and understand plots, graphs, diagrams carefully
- Note down terminology that you don't understand or questions you have

## Third pass
This pass is to fully understand the paper. It make take many hours but by the
end of the third pass, you should be able to re-implement the paper meaning you
should be able to reach the same conclusions from the same assumptions as the
author. During this pass, you should question every detail and consider
alternatives to any decisions that were taken. You should be able to understand
the fundamental brekathrough that the paper is written on.
