## Network of networks

Pages 33-34. Section 1.3.3

The internet is broken into a hierarchical network of networks. You are
typically connected to an access ISP. Access ISPs connect to regional ISPs
which connect to tier-1 ISPs. Access ISPs might even connect directly with
tier-1 ISPs. This hierarchical layering of networks can be even deeper in some
places.

- Access ISP: The ISP that provides end users with physical or wireless
connection to the internet. This could be your cable or fibre optic provider.
- Regional ISP: Your access ISP is connected to a regional ISP that is
connected to tier-1 ISPs
- Tier 1 ISP: ISPs that span over the globe and connect with multiple lower
level ISPs and other tier-1 ISPs (For example like AT&T, NTT, Sprint) 
- Points of persistence (PoP): A group of one or more routers in the provider's
network where customer ISPs can connect into the provider's ISP. Essentially
the connection point into the ISP that you are connecting to for service.
- Multi-home: connecting to two or more provider ISPs.
- Peering: ISPs at the same hierarchical level agree to directly connect
networks without having to go through a network at a higher level. Typically
done to save money.
- Internet Exchange Point (IXP): Meeting point where ISPs can peer together.
- Content provider networks: Networks operated by private entities to serve
some business need (like Google for serving content).
